# Research — Startup Book / Framework Canon (Cluster r2)

Prior-art-driven deep review of six canonical startup sources, mined for **mechanics** (canvases, question sets, decision rules) that a `startup` skill can reuse to (a) interview a user for a real, evidenced idea and (b) turn it into a detailed project design. Not headlines — the specific gears.

Sources covered: The Mom Test (Fitzpatrick), The Lean Startup (Ries), Running Lean (Maurya), Disciplined Entrepreneurship / 24 Steps (Aulet), The Four Steps to the Epiphany (Blank), Zero to One (Thiel).

**Verification legend:** `[V]` = specific mechanic verified against a web source this session (URL in Sources). `[K]` = well-established from the canon but not re-quoted from a source this session — treat exact wording as approximate, concept as reliable.

## Interview craft

The Mom Test is the load-bearing interview-craft source; the others contribute the *why* and the *what-to-probe-for*.

### The Mom Test — the whole method is "extract truth when everyone is lying to you (politely)"

- **Three rules** `[V]`: (1) *Talk about their life, not your idea* — don't pitch, don't even mention the idea; ask about the customer's world. (2) *Ask about specifics in the past, not generics or opinions about the future* — "anything about the future is an over-optimistic lie." (3) *Talk less, listen more.* (Source: Mom Test, ch. "The Mom Test" / rules.)
- **The name's logic** `[V]`: the test is a set of questions phrased so that *even your mom cannot lie to you* — because they never ask her to judge your idea, only to recount her own life. Bad questions invite comforting lies; good questions cannot be answered flatteringly. (Mom Test, intro.)
- **Three kinds of bad data** `[V]` — the interviewer must recognize and neutralize each:
  1. **Compliments** ("cool!", "I love it", "great idea") — worthless; they cost the speaker nothing. *Handle:* deflect — don't bask; ignore the praise and steer back to facts and commitments. Ending a meeting with only compliments is the failure symptom ("zombie leads").
  2. **Fluff** — generic claims ("I usually…", "I always…"), hypotheticals ("I would…"), and futures ("I will…"). Trigger words: *always / usually / never / would.* *Handle:* anchor to a concrete past instance — "talk me through the last time that happened."
  3. **Ideas / feature requests** — customers hand you solutions and features. *Handle:* don't accept at face value; dig to the underlying motivation and the problem behind the request (the request is a symptom, not a spec).
  (Source: Mom Test, ch. "Avoiding bad data"; verified via unusual.vc + search.)
- **Commitment & advancement** `[V]` — the anti-fluff safeguard. A conversation only counts if it ends with the other person *giving something up*. Measure credibility by "what they have given up," not by what they said.
  - **Three currencies of commitment, in escalating seriousness:** **Time** (a scheduled next meeting with defined objectives, sitting through a long feedback session, ongoing access), **Reputation** (an intro to peers / boss / decision-maker; a public endorsement / testimonial; also *risk* — willingness to try an immature product), **Money** (a letter of intent, a deposit, a pre-order, an actual payment).
  - **Advancement** = the prospect moves to the next step of your conversion funnel (chat → demo → trial → purchase). A meeting *succeeded* only if it ends with a commitment to advance. No commitment + no advancement = the meeting was worthless no matter how warm it felt. (Source: Mom Test, ch. 5 "Commitment and Advancement".)
- **"It's not your job to teach them, it's to learn from them"** `[K]` — the single biggest way to ruin a customer conversation is to talk about your idea; the moment you pitch, you poison the well and get compliments back. (Mom Test, ch. "Talking about your idea".)
- **Look for what they already do and already pay for** `[K]` — the strongest signal is an existing workaround or an existing spend: people who already hack a solution together, already pay for a substitute, or have an active budget line have proven the problem is real. "What are you doing now to solve this?" and "what have you already tried?" surface this. (Mom Test, ch. "Asking good questions".)
- **Prep three big questions before every conversation** `[K]` — go in with the three most important things you need to learn (your biggest risky assumptions); a conversation without pre-set learning goals drifts into pitch-and-compliment. (Mom Test, ch. "Keeping it casual" / "Choosing your questions".)

### Cross-canon interview discipline

- **Get out of the building** (Blank) `[V]` — no facts exist inside the building; founders must physically go interview real customers. A startup is "a temporary organization built to *search* for a scalable, repeatable business model," and the search happens only in front of customers. (Four Steps, Customer Development premise.)
- **Earlyvangelist profile** (Blank) `[K]` — the ideal first interviewee/customer has all five: has the problem, knows they have it, is actively searching for a solution, has cobbled together an interim fix, and can/will pay for a solution. This is the qualification filter for *whose* answers to weight. (Four Steps, Customer Discovery.) *Flag: five-part definition is from the canon, not re-quoted this session.*
- **Deflate a good idea to get truth** (Mom Test spirit + Ries) `[K]/[V]` — actively try to *disprove* your idea. Ries frames every feature/idea as a hypothesis to be falsified via a test, not confirmed via encouragement (validated learning). Seek the answer that kills the idea cheaply now. (Lean Startup, "Validated Learning".) `[V]`

## Design / idea craft

How the canon turns a validated problem into a concrete, buildable business design. Two dominant "fill-in-the-boxes" instruments: Maurya's **Lean Canvas** (fast, one-page) and Aulet's **24 Steps** (rigorous, sequential).

### Running Lean — the Lean Canvas (9 boxes) `[V]`

Nine blocks, adapted by Maurya from Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas to be startup/risk-focused:

1. **Problem** (top 1–3 problems) + **Existing Alternatives** (how the problem is solved today — the real competition).
2. **Customer Segments** + **Early Adopters** (who feels the pain most).
3. **Unique Value Proposition** — a single, clear, compelling message; "why you're different and worth attention." Maurya adds a **High-Level Concept** (an X-for-Y analogy).
4. **Solution** — deliberately kept *small* (a box, not a plan): the MVP. Maurya: keep it small so you don't fall in love with the first solution and "corner yourself into legacy."
5. **Channels** — path to customers.
6. **Revenue Streams** + **Cost Structure** (the two together = the economics box pair).
7. **Key Metrics** — the few numbers that tell you the business is working.
8. **Unfair Advantage** — the thing that "can't be easily copied or bought" (the moat box).

**Sequencing rule** `[V]`: validate in order — **Problem–Solution Fit** (do people want it? is the problem real and worth solving?) *before* **Product–Market Fit** (have you built something people want, at scale?). Business models fail because founders "assume they already know what customers want and finalize their plans before testing." Fill the *riskiest* boxes first; the canvas is a snapshot of hypotheses, not a plan. (Source: Running Lean, Lean Canvas + "Plan A → a plan that works".)

### Disciplined Entrepreneurship — 6 themes / 24 steps `[V]`

**Single necessary-and-sufficient condition of a business:** a *paying customer.* Everything else serves that.

**Six themes** (each a design question):
1. **Who is your customer?** — Steps: Market Segmentation → Select a Beachhead Market → Build an End User Profile → Calculate TAM for the beachhead → Profile the Persona → (identify next-10 customers).
2. **What can you do for your customer?** — Full Life Cycle Use Case → High-Level Product Spec → **Quantify the Value Proposition** → Define Your Core → Chart Your Competitive Position.
3. **How does the customer acquire your product?** — Determine the **Decision-Making Unit (DMU)** → Map the process/steps to acquire a paying customer → estimate sales-cycle length.
4. **How do you make money off your product?** — TAM for follow-on markets → Design a Business Model → Set a Pricing Framework → Calculate **LTV** of an acquired customer → Map the sales process → Calculate **COCA** (cost of customer acquisition).
5. **How do you design & build your product?** — key assumptions → test them → define the MVBP (minimum viable business product) → "show dogs will eat the dog food" (evidence of real adoption).
6. **How do you scale?** — TAM for follow-on markets, next markets, the scaling plan.

**Load-bearing design mechanics:**
- **Beachhead market** `[V]` — win 100% of a tiny, specific market first; dominate a narrow segment before expanding. (Directly echoes Thiel's "big share of a small market.")
- **TAM = end users × annual revenue per user** `[V]` — the annual revenue if you had 100% share of the beachhead. A concrete number, not "the market is huge."
- **Persona** `[V]` — pick ONE real end user from ONE customer who best exemplifies the End User Profile; design for that named person, not an abstraction.
- **End user vs economic buyer vs the DMU** `[V]/[K]` — separate the person who *uses* the product from the person who *pays*; the DMU includes champion, end user, primary economic buyer, plus influencers and people with veto power. You must map all of them to make a sale.
- **Quantified Value Proposition (QVP)** `[V]` — state the benefit as a *number* tied to what the customer most wants to improve (e.g., "cuts X cost by $N/yr"), aligned to the persona's top priority — not adjectives.
- **"Core" vs "competitive position"** `[K]` — your *Core* is the one thing you do that others cannot easily copy (internal, singular). Your *competitive position* is how you win on the two priorities the persona cares about most (external, relative). They are different questions. (Source: Disciplined Entrepreneurship, Steps 1–24; verified via O'Reilly/MIT Sloan.)

### The Lean Startup — the build/learn engine (design as experiment) `[V]`

- **Build–Measure–Learn** — turn ideas into products, measure customer response, learn whether to pivot or persevere; minimize *total time through the loop*.
- **Validated learning** — progress is measured by empirically demonstrated truths about the business, not by shipped features. More concrete and faster than a business plan.
- **MVP** — the smallest thing that lets you start the learning loop (not the smallest product — the smallest *experiment*).
- **Innovation accounting** (3 steps): (1) use an MVP to get real baseline data; (2) tune the engine from baseline toward the ideal via micro-changes; (3) at the decision point, **pivot or persevere.**
- **Engines of growth** (pick one): **viral**, **sticky** (retention), **paid** — each has its own key metric and its own tuning knobs. (Source: Lean Startup, Parts II–III.)

### The Four Steps — Customer Development (the search process) `[V]`

Four iterative phases (circular, not linear): **Customer Discovery** (is the problem real and does the product solve it?) → **Customer Validation** (is there a repeatable, scalable sales model / real market?) → **Customer Creation** (create/scale demand) → **Company Building** (transition from a search org to an execution org). Discovery + Validation together = the *search for product–market fit*; you will "screw it up several times before getting it right." (Source: Four Steps, Customer Development model.)

## Startup quality axes

Distilled evaluation dimensions the canon uses to judge whether an idea is *good*. These are what a `startup` interview should score an idea against.

| Axis | What "good" looks like | Canonical test / mechanic |
|---|---|---|
| **Demand (is the problem real?)** | An existing, painful, frequent problem people already spend time/money working around. | Mom Test: existing workaround + active budget; "what are you doing now to solve this?" `[V]`. Blank: earlyvangelist has already cobbled a fix `[K]`. |
| **Willingness to pay (WTP)** | Someone commits real currency; a *paying* customer exists. | Mom Test currencies (time/reputation/money) + advancement `[V]`. Aulet: "single necessary & sufficient condition = a paying customer" `[V]`. |
| **Product–Market Fit (PMF)** | Built something people want, at scale; measured, not felt. | Maurya: Problem–Solution Fit *then* PMF `[V]`. Ries: innovation accounting baseline→ideal `[V]`. |
| **Business-model coherence** | Unit economics work: **LTV > COCA**, a chosen engine of growth, a real revenue/cost structure. | Aulet LTV & COCA `[V]`; Ries engines of growth `[V]`; Lean Canvas revenue+cost boxes `[V]`. |
| **Moat / defensibility (durability)** | An unfair advantage that can't be cheaply copied or bought; position defensible in 10–20 yrs. | Lean Canvas "Unfair Advantage" `[V]`; Thiel Durability Q + monopoly traits (proprietary tech 10x better, network effects, economies of scale, brand) `[V]/[K]`; last-mover advantage `[K]`. |
| **Competition / market structure** | Start by *dominating* a small market rather than fighting in a big undifferentiated one. | Thiel Monopoly Q: "big share of a small market" `[V]`; Aulet beachhead `[V]`. Maurya: "Existing Alternatives" is the real competition `[V]`. |
| **Timing** | "Why now?" — a specific reason the window is open now. | Thiel Timing Q `[V]`. |
| **Secret / contrarian edge** | A true, important thing few people agree with or see. | Thiel Secret Q + contrarian question `[V]/[K]`. |

### Zero to One — the sharpest quality lens `[V]`

- **The contrarian question** `[K]`: "What important truth do very few people agree with you on?" — every great business is built on a secret others miss. *Flag: exact wording from the canon, not re-quoted this session.*
- **Monopoly vs competition** `[V]`: competition is for losers; aim to be a monopoly (10x better on something), because only monopolies earn durable profit. Start by owning a *small* market entirely, then expand.
- **The 7 questions every business must answer** `[V]` — nail all 7; even 5–6 can work:
  1. **Engineering** — breakthrough (10x) tech, not incremental?
  2. **Timing** — is now the right time?
  3. **Monopoly** — starting with a big share of a small market?
  4. **People** — right team?
  5. **Distribution** — a way to *deliver*, not just build?
  6. **Durability** — position defensible in 10 and 20 years?
  7. **Secret** — a unique opportunity others don't see?
- **Last-mover advantage / power law** `[K]`: the goal is to be the *last* mover — make the last great development in a market and enjoy years of monopoly profit; value follows a power law, so the durability of the moat dominates everything. (Source: Zero to One, chs. 3–5, 8, 13.)

## Load-bearing truths (each with a one-line why)

1. **A prompt/idea from a founder is an over-optimistic hypothesis, not a fact.** — Why: the Mom Test's whole premise is that direct feedback about *your idea* is systematically distorted; the interview must extract truth indirectly. `[V]`
2. **Never pitch during discovery; ask about the person's life and past.** — Why: mentioning the idea invites compliments and poisons the data (Mom Test rule 1). `[V]`
3. **Past behavior beats future intention.** — Why: "anything about the future is an over-optimistic lie"; anchor fluff to a concrete last-time instance. `[V]`
4. **Belief is worthless without a commitment in a real currency (time/reputation/money).** — Why: credibility = "what they gave up"; a warm meeting with no advancement is a failure. `[V]`
5. **Existing workarounds and active budgets are the strongest demand signal.** — Why: people already hacking or already paying have proven the problem is real (Mom Test + earlyvangelist). `[V]/[K]`
6. **Validate Problem–Solution Fit before Product–Market Fit; treat every idea as a falsifiable experiment.** — Why: models fail when founders finalize plans before testing (Maurya, Ries validated learning). `[V]`
7. **The single necessary-and-sufficient condition of a business is a paying customer.** — Why: Aulet's anchor keeps the design honest — every step serves converting one paying customer. `[V]`
8. **Win 100% of a tiny beachhead before expanding.** — Why: dominating a narrow segment builds a base and a reference story; big undifferentiated markets are traps (Aulet beachhead = Thiel "big share of a small market"). `[V]`
9. **Separate the end user from the economic buyer, and map the whole decision-making unit.** — Why: the person who loves the product often isn't the person who pays or can veto the purchase (Aulet DMU). `[V]/[K]`
10. **State value as a quantified number tied to the persona's top priority, not adjectives.** — Why: a QVP ("saves $N/yr") is testable and sellable; "better/faster/cheaper" is not (Aulet QVP). `[V]`
11. **Unit economics decide viability: LTV must exceed COCA, on a named engine of growth.** — Why: a beloved product with LTV < COCA is not a business (Aulet + Ries engines). `[V]`
12. **Defensibility is a first-class design question, not an afterthought.** — Why: without an unfair advantage / moat, any success is competed away (Lean Canvas Unfair Advantage; Thiel Durability + monopoly). `[V]`
13. **"Why now?" must have a specific answer.** — Why: timing is one of Thiel's 7 and separates a good idea from a good idea *too early/late*. `[V]`
14. **A great idea rests on a contrarian secret — something true that few believe or see.** — Why: if everyone already agrees, the market is efficient and the opportunity is gone (Thiel). `[V]/[K]`
15. **The startup's job is to SEARCH for a business model, not execute a known one — so the process is iterative and you will be wrong first.** — Why: Blank's core reframing; design must expect pivots (Ries pivot-or-persevere), not a one-shot plan. `[V]`

### Convergences & tensions (for the synthesis leader)

- **Strong convergence:** beachhead (Aulet) ≡ "big share of a small market" (Thiel) ≡ early-adopters box (Maurya) ≡ earlyvangelist (Blank) — *narrow first* is unanimous.
- **Strong convergence:** problem-first, evidence-before-build — Mom Test, Running Lean, Four Steps, Lean Startup all agree; only Thiel weights *secret/tech/monopoly* more than iterative discovery.
- **Tension (useful, not a contradiction):** Ries/Blank/Maurya optimize for *fast iterative search* (lots of small bets, cheap tests). Thiel is skeptical of pure iteration and argues for a *bold definite plan* built on a secret + 10x tech. A `startup` skill should hold both: iterate to find the problem, but force a durability/secret answer so the idea isn't merely a local optimum.
- **Tension:** Aulet's 24 Steps is heavy/sequential; Maurya's Lean Canvas is light/parallel. For an interview→design skill, Lean Canvas is the better *live scaffold* (fillable in one session); the 24 Steps is the better *depth checklist* for the design write-up.

## Directly reusable questions

Verbatim or near-verbatim probing questions the skill can lift. Tagged **[INT]** = customer/founder interview (discovery), **[DES]** = design/idea-shaping (turning the validated idea into a project design).

### Interview (discovery) — Mom Test style

- **[INT]** "Talk me through the last time that happened." `[V]` — converts fluff into a concrete story.
- **[INT]** "What are you doing now to solve this?" `[K]` — surfaces existing workaround = real demand.
- **[INT]** "What else have you tried?" / "What have you already tried?" `[K]` — reveals substitutes and prior spend.
- **[INT]** "How are you dealing with it now?" `[V]` — current-state behavior, not opinion.
- **[INT]** "Why do you bother?" `[V]` — digs to the real underlying motivation behind a stated problem.
- **[INT]** "What are the implications of that?" `[V]` — separates a costly, must-solve problem from a minor annoyance.
- **[INT]** "How much does that problem cost you?" (time or money) `[K]` — quantifies pain; feeds WTP.
- **[INT]** "Where does the budget for this come from?" / "Who controls that budget?" `[K]` — finds the economic buyer / DMU.
- **[INT]** "Who else should I talk to?" `[K]` — the ask that itself tests commitment (reputation currency = an intro).
- **[INT]** "What would you need to see to [try it / buy it]?" `[K]` — sets up an advancement / commitment ask.
- **[INT-avoid]** BAD questions the skill must NOT ask: "Do you think it's a good idea?", "Would you buy a product that did X?", "How much would you pay for X?" — all invite compliments/hypotheticals. `[V]` (fluff + future = bad data).

### Design / idea-shaping — from the framework canon

- **[DES]** "Who is the ONE persona — a real, named end user — who feels this most?" `[V]` (Aulet Persona).
- **[DES]** "Who is the end user, who is the economic buyer, and who else is in the decision-making unit (champion / influencers / veto)?" `[V]/[K]` (Aulet DMU).
- **[DES]** "What is the smallest market you could win 100% of first (the beachhead)?" `[V]` (Aulet + Thiel).
- **[DES]** "What is the TAM = (number of end users) × (annual revenue per user) for that beachhead?" `[V]` (Aulet TAM).
- **[DES]** "State the value as a number: how much does it save/earn the persona per year, on the priority they care about most?" `[V]` (Aulet QVP).
- **[DES]** "What are the existing alternatives — how is this solved today?" `[V]` (Maurya Lean Canvas: real competition).
- **[DES]** "What is your Unfair Advantage — the thing that can't be easily copied or bought?" `[V]` (Maurya Unfair Advantage / moat).
- **[DES]** "What is the ONE riskiest assumption, and what is the cheapest experiment (MVP) that could disprove it?" `[V]` (Ries validated learning / MVP).
- **[DES]** "Which engine of growth — viral, sticky, or paid — and does LTV exceed COCA on it?" `[V]` (Ries + Aulet unit economics).
- **[DES]** "Why now? What changed that opens this window?" `[V]` (Thiel Timing).
- **[DES]** "What important truth do very few people agree with you on?" `[K]` (Thiel contrarian question).
- **[DES]** "Will this position be defensible in 10 and 20 years?" `[V]` (Thiel Durability).
- **[DES]** Run the idea through Thiel's 7: Engineering / Timing / Monopoly / People / Distribution / Durability / Secret. `[V]`
